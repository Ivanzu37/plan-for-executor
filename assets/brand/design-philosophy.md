# Relay Geometry

Two marks, one direction. Relay Geometry is the discipline of saying a transfer with the fewest possible shapes: a large form that decides, a smaller form that carries on, and the exact gap between them where responsibility changes hands. Nothing else is allowed on the field. Every element that is not the transfer is removed, and what remains is measured until it cannot be moved by a single unit without getting worse.

Form is stroke, not outline. Each mark is one continuous line of constant weight, bent once, with rounded ends so the line reads as drawn by a steady hand rather than cut by a machine. Weight carries hierarchy: the leading mark is heavier and taller, the following mark lighter and shorter, and the ratio between them is tuned by eye, by countless small corrections, until the second feels like a consequence of the first rather than a copy.

Color is a single warm field and a single light. The field is a fired, earthen orange, deep enough that white sits on it with real contrast, warm enough to feel human. White is the only other value. No gradients, no shadows, no glints: depth comes from proportion and spacing alone, which is why every distance is meticulously calibrated.

Scale is the test of honesty. The composition must survive being reduced to sixteen pixels and still be the same idea: two bright strokes pointing forward. Anything that only works large is decoration and is cut. The negative space inside each bend and the gap between the marks are drawn with the same painstaking attention as the marks themselves, because at small sizes the space is the shape.

Balance is optical, not arithmetic. Forms that point forward lean the eye forward, so the group sits a breath behind the geometric centre to feel centred. Corners of the field are soft and generous, the margins wide enough that the marks never feel crowded by their container. The result should look inevitable, the product of deep expertise and master-level restraint: simple enough to be remembered after one glance, exact enough to reward a long one.
